Site read · evidence-informed inference

What the field is asking of a six.

These are preparation conclusions drawn from the usage patterns and successful public team shells below. They are prompts for testing, not blanket matchup guarantees.

01 · RAIN HAS LAYERS

Do not prep for one rain lead.

Pelipper, Archaludon, and Basculegion make the standard rain damage plan common, but Gengar / Politoed / Vivillon branches add trapping, Perish Song, sleep, and redirection. A single electric check is not a full rain plan.

WATSTLGHO
02 · ENDGAMES STAY SHARP

Steel / Dark plus Ghost pressure is the baseline.

Kingambit and Basculegion are the top two Pokémon in the newest snapshot, while Incineroar remains the broad-event pivot. Test whether your endgame holds when priority, Intimidate cycling, and Ghost damage arrive together.

DRKSTLGHO
03 · SPEED IS DISTRIBUTED

Beat the plan you need to beat.

Sneasler, Farigiraf, Whimsicott, Grimmsnarl, weather, and priority denial distribute speed control across the field. The useful question is usually not “do I beat every tier?” but “which speed plan is my team designed to beat?”

FIGPSYFRY
Branch to watch · current field signal

Perish Rain has a more complete support package.

Current public-team evidence shows a Gengar / Politoed / Archaludon / Mega Swampert / Incineroar / Vivillon six. It can pivot between rain damage, Shadow Tag + Perish Song turns, Vivillon disruption, and a conventional Steel / Water endgame.

Prep checklist

Four questions worth asking in builder.

1
Can I close through Basculegion?Account for Water / Ghost damage, not only its speed.
2
Is my Grass pressure protected?It is the clean Swampert axis, but it has to survive the support around it.
3
Do I have a non-single-mode rain plan?Test both conventional rain and Perish / disruption lines.
4
What is my chosen speed benchmark?Build for relevant speed control rather than sacrificing bulk to chase every number.

Method guardrail.

Field Pulse uses published tournament teams and usage as observation, then labels the strength of the evidence beside every event. The five high-weight completed events total 1,952 entries and drive the guide’s tier decisions. Fresh small samples can raise a watchlist flag but cannot, by themselves, promote a Pokémon or archetype.
