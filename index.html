Charizard / Floette / Whimsicott Big Six

Signal: Official, Arena II, VR July

IDENTITY

Tailwind offense with immediate Fairy pressure, sun-enabled spread damage, and a preserved Basculegion or Kingambit endgame.

◇ Common variations

  • Incineroar over Kingambit for Intimidate, Fake Out, and safer Charizard/Floette positioning.
  • Dragonite, Sneasler, Hisuian Arcanine, Gholdengo, or Sinistcha can replace a standard slot to alter the matchup spread.
  • Calm Mind Floette variants trade burst damage for a slower, supported win condition.

↗ Lead / mode tree

  • 1Whimsicott + Floette: speed plus immediate Light of Ruin pressure.
  • 2Whimsicott + Garchomp: Tailwind with fast physical spread pressure.
  • 3Charizard or Basculegion is frequently preserved to close after early trades.

◎ Preparation priorities

  • 1Do not spend every resource removing Whimsicott while leaving its partner unchecked.
  • 2Have a late-game Ghost/Dark answer after Tailwind expires.
  • 3Respect Encore, Charm, Focus Sash, and the second Mega at preview.

IDENTITY

A speed-control fork: Tailwind and immediate sun offense on one branch, Armor Tail plus Trick Room or slower Fairy pressure on the other.

◇ Common variations

  • Incineroar can replace Kingambit for pivoting; Kingambit improves Basculegion and Trick Room matchups.
  • Venusaur or Watchog can appear to strengthen sun and disruption.
  • Mega Mawile is a credible alternative Mega on slower Farigiraf versions.

↗ Lead / mode tree

  • 1Aerodactyl + Charizard threatens fast spread damage and Tailwind.
  • 2Farigiraf + Sylveon creates a protected slower mode.
  • 3Garchomp is the bridge piece that checks opposing Charizard and punishes Rock/Steel answers.

◎ Preparation priorities

  • 1Identify the likely Mega rather than assuming Charizard.
  • 2Do not make priority your only defensive plan into Armor Tail.
  • 3Plan for both Rock Slide pressure and Trick Room sequencing.

Signal: VR July winner and runner-up variants

IDENTITY

Fast, branch-heavy offense built around Contrary Mega Staraptor, Whimsicott manipulation, and a second Mega that punishes anti-Staraptor positioning.

◇ Common variations

  • Mega Raichu Y + Farigiraf + Sylveon + Hisuian Arcanine is a more balanced version.
  • Alolan Ninetales + Gholdengo + Sinistcha + Hisuian Arcanine creates a screens-and-attrition version.
  • Staraptor also slots directly into sand, turning the archetype into a hybrid weather offense.

↗ Lead / mode tree

  • 1Whimsicott + Staraptor threatens Tailwind, self-Charm, or immediate Brave Bird / Close Combat.
  • 2Whimsicott + Delphox attacks the anti-Flying and Steel answers.
  • 3Kingambit stabilizes priority, Psychic, Basculegion, and Trick Room endgames.

◎ Preparation priorities

  • 1Avoid Intimidate, Parting Shot, Scary Face, Charm, or Fake Tears into Mega Staraptor.
  • 2Use burn, poison, Haze, Ghost/Steel resistances, or immediate speed-safe damage.
  • 3Treat Focus Sash Whimsicott as a two-action problem, not a guaranteed one-hit removal.

IDENTITY

A three-layer physical structure: weather control, Sand Rush pressure, and a Flying/Fighting Mega that is immune to Excadrill Earthquake.

◇ Common variations

  • The exact six has repeated at multiple events, which makes set and lead familiarity especially valuable.
  • Lycanroc or Farigiraf can replace the slower stabilizers for more immediate pressure.
  • Non-Mega Tyranitar plus an alternative Mega is possible, so the weather setter is not always the power slot.

↗ Lead / mode tree

  • 1Tyranitar + Excadrill applies classic weather and spread pressure.
  • 2Staraptor + Sinistcha or Milotic plays a slower, defensive-boosting mode.
  • 3Gholdengo punishes passive Fairy, status, and Fighting counterplay.

◎ Preparation priorities

  • 1Answer Tyranitar/Excadrill without leaving Staraptor uncontested.
  • 2Do not rely on Rock attacks being super effective into Mega Staraptor.
  • 3Preserve a Ground immunity and a non-stat-based Staraptor answer.

Signal: Encore winner; repeated VR July finishes
MEGA
Charizard
Venusaur

IDENTITY

A six that changes identity mid-game: sun pressure, rain-enabled Archaludon, and Basculegion endgames are connected by Grimmsnarl and weather switching.

◇ Common variations

  • Some versions use Scovillain or Floette as the alternative Mega.
  • Mega Swampert can replace Basculegion or Charizard to create a more committed rain branch.
  • The exact Encore six remains common enough to merit set-specific preparation.

↗ Lead / mode tree

  • 1Charizard + Venusaur creates immediate sun offense.
  • 2Pelipper + Archaludon creates rain setup and Electro Shot pressure.
  • 3Grimmsnarl Parting Shot changes weather safely; Basculegion closes after the weather war.

◎ Preparation priorities

  • 1Do not assume sun makes Solar Beam safe; Pelipper can reverse the field instantly.
  • 2Track weather setters and Mega timing, not only current weather.
  • 3Keep a concrete Archaludon and late Basculegion plan.

IDENTITY

Dual-Mega setup balance: Nasty Plot Delphox and Shell Smash Blastoise share redirection, Hospitality, Fake Out, and Kingambit endgame support.

◇ Common variations

  • Mega Floette can replace Blastoise for a Fairy-heavy setup mode.
  • Maushold or Vivillon may replace Kingambit for redirection or sleep pressure.
  • Some versions retain the exact five-piece Incineroar / Sinistcha / Sneasler shell while changing only the second Mega.

↗ Lead / mode tree

  • 1Incineroar or Sneasler buys the setup turn.
  • 2Sinistcha redirects, heals Water Spout damage, and extends the setup threat.
  • 3Kingambit punishes Psychic, Intimidate, and over-defensive responses.

◎ Preparation priorities

  • 1Bring Taunt, Haze, phazing, immediate double-target pressure, or a way to deny the setup turn.
  • 2Do not let Sinistcha turn a damaged Blastoise back into a full-power Water Spout threat.
  • 3Distinguish which Mega is actually dangerous in the specific preview.

IDENTITY

Bulky sun balance that trades some speed for Toxapex control, Venusaur longevity, Intimidate cycling, and a physically resilient endgame.

◇ Common variations

  • Sylveon can replace Annihilape for stronger Fairy pressure.
  • Hydreigon, Basculegion, Whimsicott, or Kingambit can replace the final support slot.
  • Toxapex may run Infestation, Haze, Toxic, Wide Guard, and Baneful Bunker in different combinations.

↗ Lead / mode tree

  • 1Charizard + Garchomp remains the immediate offensive mode.
  • 2Venusaur + Toxapex creates a slower resistance-and-status board.
  • 3Annihilape or Sylveon punishes opponents who focus exclusively on the sun core.

◎ Preparation priorities

  • 1Do not attack Toxapex automatically; identify whether its partner is the real win condition.
  • 2Bring single-target Ground, Psychic, Electric, Taunt, or setup that ignores Toxic.
  • 3Account for Wide Guard before relying on spread attacks.

IDENTITY

Immediate, high-speed offense with snow or screen pressure, Fake Out / Quick Guard branches, and several ways to win move order on turn one.

◇ Common variations

  • Mega Scovillain + Lycanroc + Basculegion is the established alternative shell.
  • Dragonite + Gholdengo + Hisuian Arcanine adds a sturdier balance route.
  • Toxapex + Blaziken converts the shell into a slower tempo-trap version.

↗ Lead / mode tree

  • 1Froslass can Mega to set snow, enable Aurora Veil, or simply pressure with fast Ice damage.
  • 2Sneasler and Raichu create layered Fake Out, priority, and redirection decisions.
  • 3Kingambit anchors the game if the first offensive wave trades down.

◎ Preparation priorities

  • 1Have a plan for Focus Sash and weather/screen denial.
  • 2Do not assume Fake Out solves the lead because Quick Guard and opposing Fake Out are common.
  • 3Preserve one bulky endgame piece after surviving the first two turns.

IDENTITY

A slower Floette family that replaces pure Tailwind racing with redirection, recovery, pivoting, and Calm Mind or supported Mega pressure.

◇ Common variations

  • Mega Garchomp, Delphox, Blastoise, Raichu, Ceruledge, or Milotic can occupy the second Mega/offensive slot.
  • Sneasler may be offensive or heavily supportive with Fake Out and Quick Guard.
  • Incineroar and Kingambit are interchangeable depending on whether pivoting or anti-Intimidate pressure is prioritized.

↗ Lead / mode tree

  • 1Sinistcha protects and heals the chosen Mega.
  • 2Incineroar and Sneasler create the setup window.
  • 3Kingambit provides the low-speed endgame that prevents the team from being entirely tempo-dependent.

◎ Preparation priorities

  • 1Deny redirection and healing before the setup threat becomes permanent.
  • 2Keep a Steel or Poison answer healthy for Floette.
  • 3Do not overtrade into Hospitality and Draining Kiss recovery.

Signal: Official overview; Arena II and OCL3

IDENTITY

Shadow Tag positioning: either Perish Song control or a balance trap that locks vulnerable targets beside Fake Out, Disable, Encore, sleep, or setup.

◇ Common variations

  • Perish versions commonly use Politoed as a second song user.
  • Balance versions pair Gengar with Alolan Ninetales, Floette, Kingambit, or Sneasler.
  • Rain variants use Swampert and Archaludon so the trap is not the only win condition.

↗ Lead / mode tree

  • 1Trap two poor targets, then cycle Incineroar/Sinistcha while the Perish count advances.
  • 2Encore + Disable can force Struggle or an unusable move loop.
  • 3Rain or setup offense punishes opponents who overprepare for Perish.

◎ Preparation priorities

  • 1Bring Ghost-types or immediate Gengar pressure.
  • 2Avoid giving Shadow Tag two targets that both need to switch.
  • 3Track Perish counts, Protect turns, and the second song user.

IDENTITY

Armor Tail-backed speed inversion. Semiroom versions keep a normal fast mode; hard-room versions devote more slots to slow sweepers and setup Megas.

◇ Common variations

  • Aero/Zard/Farigiraf is the premier semiroom shell.
  • Mega Mawile, Torkoal, Hatterene, Mamoswine, Camerupt, or Abomasnow appear on harder Trick Room.
  • Raichu/Farigiraf balance uses Fake Out and an alternative Mega rather than committing fully to room.

↗ Lead / mode tree

  • 1Farigiraf protects setup from priority and can reverse opposing speed control.
  • 2Kingambit and Sylveon are useful both inside and outside Trick Room.
  • 3Hard-room teams may use redirection or sleep rather than a second fast mode.

◎ Preparation priorities

  • 1Carry non-priority Dark/Ghost pressure and a plan if Trick Room succeeds.
  • 2Do not rely entirely on Fake Out or Sucker Punch into Armor Tail.
  • 3Know which of your own Pokémon should be preserved as the slow-mode answer.

MEGA

IDENTITY

A tempo trap: Toxapex makes attacks and Protect turns inefficient while Speed Boost Blaziken or another fast Mega converts the hesitation into damage.

◇ Common variations

  • Mega Venusaur, Alolan Ninetales, Lycanroc, or Basculegion can change the weather and matchup profile.
  • Hydreigon is a natural addition when Ground and Psychic pressure overload the original shell.
  • Toxapex may use Haze to reset Staraptor and setup teams instead of Infestation.

↗ Lead / mode tree

  • 1Fake Out or screens buy Blaziken its first Speed Boost.
  • 2Wide Guard, Baneful Bunker, and Toxic punish repetitive physical offense.
  • 3Kingambit remains the emergency endgame when the support pieces trade slowly.

◎ Preparation priorities

  • 1Pressure Ground/Psychic weaknesses with single-target attacks.
  • 2Taunt or ignore Toxapex and remove its partner.
  • 3Keep priority or weather chip for a low-health boosted Blaziken.

Metagame movement

What changed from Encore
to Victory Road.

The field widened without becoming random. Repeated packages still matter, but Mega ambiguity and Staraptor now punish narrow Charizard-only preparation.

01

Charizard consolidated

Encore’s benchmark shells remain viable, but they no longer monopolize top-cut conversion.

02

Staraptor rose

Contrary Staraptor now appears across Whimsicott offense, sand, screens, and mode-flex balance.

03

Dual-Mega is standard

Preview must account for two distinct four-Pokémon plans, not merely an alternate item holder.

04

Kingambit stabilizes

Fast offense, Trick Room, Floette, screens, and balance use it to preserve endgame structure.

05

Packages repeat

The field is diverse, but exact or near-exact sixes recur often enough to reward targeted preparation.

06

Prepare by mode

The useful question is lead + Mega + speed plan + closer—not whether one species is present.
