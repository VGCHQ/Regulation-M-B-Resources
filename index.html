The cleanest persistent benchmark in the six-event sample. Charizard and Aerodactyl create two different Mega and speed-pressure branches, Farigiraf changes priority and speed interactions, and Garchomp plus Sylveon or Kingambit supplies the closing damage.

◇Common variations

  • Incineroar commonly replaces Kingambit for Fake Out, Intimidate, and lower-risk positioning; the Arena II-winning six used that branch.
  • Encore’s fifth-place version retained Kingambit, making the endgame slower but harder to trade through.
  • Aerodactyl may function as the Mega attacker or as a support piece beside Mega Charizard Y, so species preview alone does not reveal the mode.

↗Lead / mode tree

  • 1Aerodactyl + Charizard: immediate Rock, sun, and speed-pressure questions.
  • 2Farigiraf + Garchomp or Sylveon: priority denial with a stable damage pair.
  • 3Incineroar or Kingambit + a protected attacker: the lower-variance pivot or endgame line.

◎Preparation priorities

  • 1Decide which Mega branch your four can cover before committing the lead.
  • 2Keep answers to both Garchomp’s Ground pressure and Sylveon’s Fairy spread damage; one resistance rarely covers both.
  • 3Respect Farigiraf’s priority denial and possible speed reversal before relying on Fake Out or priority cleanup.

The defining early-format offense that remained relevant after the M-B expansion: Tailwind, immediate Fairy pressure, sun-enabled damage, and a preserved Basculegion or Kingambit endgame behind two credible Mega choices.

◇Common variations

  • Incineroar may replace Kingambit to add Fake Out, Intimidate, and safer Mega positioning.
  • Sneasler, Sinistcha, Dragonite, Gholdengo, or Hisuian Arcanine can replace a standard slot and change the matchup texture.
  • Floette can be played as immediate damage or as a slower supported win condition, so do not infer its set from the shell alone.

↗Lead / mode tree

  • 1Whimsicott + Floette: speed control beside immediate Fairy pressure.
  • 2Whimsicott + Garchomp: Tailwind with fast physical spread pressure.
  • 3Charizard, Basculegion, or Kingambit is often preserved to convert after the first two trades.

◎Preparation priorities

  • 1Do not spend every early resource removing Whimsicott while its partner takes the board.
  • 2Have a plan for Basculegion or Kingambit after Tailwind expires.
  • 3Respect Encore, Charm, Focus Sash, and the second Mega when choosing a supposedly safe Turn 1.

  • Maushold can replace Kingambit or Incineroar to increase protection and setup access.
  • Floette sometimes replaces Delphox or appears beside Blastoise, creating a Fairy-centered version of the same support kernel.

  • 2Do not allow Fake Out plus Sinistcha to purchase multiple uncontested setup turns.
  • 3Preserve a way to pressure both bulky Water and Fire/Psychic targets without giving Kingambit a free endgame.

A genuine dual-weather structure rather than a rain team with a decorative Charizard. Screens and pivoting let it choose between sun pressure, rain-enabled Archaludon/Basculegion offense, or deliberate weather cycling; the exact six occupied four places in Encore’s Top 8.

◇Common variations

  • Mega Swampert, Mega Metagross, or Sinistcha can replace the Charizard/Venusaur branch in more conventional screens rain.
  • Venusaur can be brought with Charizard, used as a utility bridge between modes, or omitted entirely for the rain four.
  • Grimmsnarl’s Parting Shot does not set weather, but it creates the safe switch that lets Charizard or Pelipper reset it.

↗Lead / mode tree

  • 1Grimmsnarl + an abuser: screens or speed disruption before the weather choice is revealed.
  • 2Pelipper + Archaludon or Basculegion: immediate rain pressure.
  • 3Charizard + Venusaur: the sun branch when Grass speed and Fire damage are the cleaner route.

◎Preparation priorities

  • 1Do not declare the matchup “rain” or “sun” from preview; identify the selected four and the weather-reset sequence.
  • 2Preserve either your weather control or a neutral line that functions under both weathers.
  • 3Coverage must answer Archaludon and Basculegion without becoming free positioning for Charizard or Venusaur.

Bulky sun that trades the pure speed of Big Six for denial, recovery, and two patient win conditions. Toxapex makes setup and careless targeting unreliable, while Annihilape or Garchomp punishes passive turns and Charizard/Venusaur controls the weather branch.

◇Common variations

  • Farigiraf, Sylveon, or Mamoswine appeared in precursor Charizard balance before the exact Encore shell consolidated.
  • Venusaur may be the second Mega option rather than merely a sun abuser.
  • Annihilape can be played as a protected closer or as the piece that punishes opponents for trying to ignore Toxapex.

↗Lead / mode tree

  • 1Incineroar + Venusaur or Charizard: safe weather positioning with Fake Out support.
  • 2Toxapex + Annihilape or Garchomp: a slower board that tests whether the opponent can make progress.
  • 3Charizard + Garchomp: immediate mixed spread pressure when denial is unnecessary.

◎Preparation priorities

  • 1Do not make setup your only route through Toxapex; plan for Haze or simple denial even when the exact move is unrevealed.
  • 2Pressure Toxapex without feeding Annihilape free turns or letting Incineroar cycle indefinitely.
  • 3Track sun turns and preserve a reliable way to damage both Venusaur and the Ground/Fighting closer.

The most reusable balance kernel in the sample. Floette supplies Fairy pressure, Sneasler creates immediate contact and Fake Out pressure, Sinistcha plus Incineroar controls board position, and Kingambit/Garchomp supplies the physical endgame.

◇Common variations

  • Basculegion, Milotic, Palafin, or Tinkaton can replace the Ground or Dark slot and change how the team punishes Intimidate.
  • Delphox, Talonflame, or Charizard can replace a standard slot to add speed or weather pressure.
  • Exact-six recurrence is lower than the kernel’s recurrence; prepare for the four-piece package, then identify the two matchup-specific attachments.

↗Lead / mode tree

  • 1Sneasler + Floette: immediate pressure with Fake Out and Fairy damage.
  • 2Incineroar + Sinistcha: a low-risk pivot lead that reveals very little about the back two.
  • 3Garchomp or Kingambit + support: the direct physical mode when the opponent overprepares for Floette.

◎Preparation priorities

  • 1Distinguish immediate-damage Floette from a supported setup version before trading your Steel answer.
  • 2Avoid automatic Intimidate or Parting Shot sequencing into Kingambit and possible Milotic branches.
  • 3Break the Incineroar/Sinistcha cycle without losing the resource needed for the final physical attacker.

A repeated exact-six offense built around two very different Mega attackers, fast disruption, and several independent cleaners. It can win before the opponent stabilizes, but it is not a single gimmick: Basculegion, Kingambit, Sneasler, and Lycanroc each punish a different defensive shortcut.

◇Common variations

  • The exact six reached repeated major Top Cuts, which makes its preview and lead trees worth memorizing rather than treating it as generic offense.
  • Garchomp or Dragonite can appear in adjacent Froslass shells, but those teams usually trade Scovillain’s Fire/Grass branch for a different closer.
  • Froslass and Scovillain can both be credible Mega selections; the matchup often determines which one is the actual centerpiece.

↗Lead / mode tree

  • 1Froslass + Sneasler: immediate speed and disruption pressure.
  • 2Scovillain + Basculegion: complementary Grass/Fire and Water/Ghost offense.
  • 3Lycanroc-Dusk + Kingambit: priority, Rock pressure, and a hard physical close.

◎Preparation priorities

  • 1Do not rely on one “Mega answer” to cover both Froslass and Scovillain.
  • 2Plan around Focus Sash and priority rather than assuming a clean one-hit knockout sequence.
  • 3Choose which cleaner must be denied first; trying to preserve perfect answers to all four attackers usually loses tempo.

  • 1Do not auto-click Intimidate or Parting Shot into Mega Staraptor; Contrary can turn the intended control into a boost.
  • 2Have a plan for Excadrill under sand rather than relying on a normally faster attacker.
  • 3Ground coverage is strong but Staraptor’s immunity means targeting order matters.

A durable fast-offense family that layers Froslass disruption with Dragonite priority, Sneasler tempo, and a Basculegion/Kingambit close. Its pieces are individually familiar; the difficulty is that several different attackers can be preserved as the final win condition.

◇Common variations

  • Garchomp commonly replaces Milotic and turns the team toward immediate spread damage.

  • 2Froslass + Dragonite: speed control beside priority or immediate Dragon/Flying damage.

  • 1Determine which of Dragonite, Basculegion, or Kingambit is being preserved before sacrificing your corresponding answer.
  • 2Respect overlapping priority and Focus Sash lines when mapping knockout order.
  • 3Do not assume removing Froslass ends speed control; the remaining five can still trade aggressively without it.
